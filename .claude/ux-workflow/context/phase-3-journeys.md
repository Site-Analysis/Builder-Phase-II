## Journey 1 — Architecture Student / Thesis Researcher

### Current State (pre-SAT)

Trigger: Site assigned for thesis or studio project

1. Physical site visit | Tool: Notebooks, measuring equipment, camera | Pain: Sensory data has no digital equivalent — physical presence always required (OBS-12)
2. Government office visits | Tool: None — physical presence required | Pain: Data frequently unavailable or credential-blocked for students (OBS-03, OBS-05)
3. Online research — topography and soil | Tool: KSRSAC, Bhuvan (via professor credentials), GSI | Pain: 20+ maps per district, no direct search, colour-matching manual (OBS-24); credential access blocked (OBS-19)
4. Climate and solar data collection | Tool: Ventrysky, Shadow Map, AcuWeather, IMD, Andrew Marsh | Pain: Recency unknown (OBS-11); too technically complex for non-specialist (OBS-06); reliability of tools unclear (OBS-18)
5. Cross-referencing and layering | Tool: Manual — no tool supports unified view | Pain: "We cannot look at them as individual entities" (OBS-09); inconsistencies surface only here (OBS-10)
6. Graphical representation | Preparation tools: AutoCAD, Revit, SketchUp | Representation tools: Adobe Photoshop, Procreate, Canva, Microsoft PowerPoint, Adobe InDesign (varies by student) | Pain: Second full manual effort after analysis is complete — different software, different skill (OBS-21, OBS-23) | SME-confirmed: Ranjitha, 2026-06-11

Output: Site analysis document for professor
Time: 6–7 weeks working alone (OBS-35)

### SAT-Assisted State

Trigger: Same

1. Physical site visit | SAT: Mobile app — guided photo capture, geotagging, audio notes uploaded directly to project | Physical presence still irreplaceable (OBS-12)
2. Open site in SAT | SAT: Unified dashboard — Topography | Climate (Sun Path, Wind, Rainfall) | Regulations (OBS-29)
3. Authoritative layered data | SAT: Sources cited per dataset; cross-layer view simultaneous (resolves OBS-09)
4. AI assistant — compliance queries | SAT: Conversational answers to regulation and climate questions (OBS-54, OBS-55)
5. Generate report | SAT: Infographic export — data + layout + citations in one step (removes current Step 6)

Time saved: Data collection (weeks → hours); representation (days → one export)

2. Site visit (multiple) | Tool: Camera, notebooks | Pain: 9 visits for single site to cover three temporal windows (OBS-34) | [ASSUMED: travel overhead not quantified in transcripts]
3. Climate and flood data | Tool: Google search — top results only | Pain: "Few websites show different and other websites show different" (OBS-47); real-time data missing (OBS-43)
4. Environmental parametric analysis | Tool: SketchUp plugins, Rhino + Grasshopper with AI-generated Python (OBS-37, OBS-38) | Pain: Requires paid licences and scripting knowledge
5. Regulatory data | Tool: Firm's government contacts — relationships not portals (OBS-49) | Pain: Only accessible via personal relationships; no digital source authoritative (OBS-48)

7. Presentation assembly | Preparation tools: AutoCAD, Revit, SketchUp | Representation tools: Adobe Photoshop, Procreate, Canva, Microsoft PowerPoint, Adobe InDesign | Pain: Graphical representation is a full separate effort requiring different software and different skills from analysis (OBS-44) | SME-confirmed: Ranjitha, 2026-06-11

Time: ~1 week (firm, parallelised); 6–7 weeks solo (OBS-35)

### SAT-Assisted State

Trigger: Same

1. Pre-study | SAT: Regulations, setbacks, FAR, height restrictions loaded with cited sources — no government contact required
2. Site visit (multiple) | SAT: Mobile app — guided photos, geotagged audio, direct project upload | Multiple visits still required (OBS-33, OBS-34)

4. AI assistant | SAT: "Would X floors violate the setback?" / "What climate risks apply to steel here?" answered in context (OBS-55)

## Journey 3 — Senior Architect / Business and Product Mentor

### Current State (pre-SAT)

Trigger: Project brief received; site assigned to team and self

1. Institutional pre-study | Tool: Internal firm archive (OBS-57) | Pain: Only works for familiar geographies — unfamiliar sites have no shortcut
2. Personal site visits (multiple) | Tool: Direct observation, camera, notes | Pain: Digital tools cannot support or extend this process — design insight from deep site observation is compressed into days with no augmentation (SME-confirmed, Ranjitha 2026-06-10)
3. Regulatory data acquisition | Tool: Firm's government contacts and legal team (OBS-49) | Pain: Only works for geographies with existing relationships
4. Team data review | Tool: Judgement and cross-referencing | Pain: Four different wind readings for same site (OBS-63); contour errors; missing regulations for unfamiliar geographies (OBS-63)
5. Material specification sign-off | Tool: Professional experience — no integrated climate-material risk tool | Pain: Decisions without real-time climate data → rework costs shift to client (OBS-45, OBS-46)
6. Client and regulatory sign-off | Tool: Manually assembled presentation | Pain: Must cite authoritative sources — "top Google result" not acceptable to review boards (OBS-65) | [ASSUMED: client presentation format not described directly in transcripts]

Output: Approved site analysis; design brief; client presentation

### SAT-Assisted State

Trigger: Same

1. Institutional pre-study | SAT: Authoritative baseline for any geography, not just familiar ones
2. Personal site visits (multiple) | SAT: Mobile app augments — guided photos, geotagged audio, feeding site intelligence layer | Personal visits remain non-negotiable (SME-confirmed)
3. Regulatory data | SAT: Bylaws, setbacks, FAR, buffer zones from cited authoritative sources — no government contact required for unfamiliar geographies
4. Team consistency | SAT: All team members source from same authoritative dataset — contradictory readings eliminated at source
5. Material risk | SAT: Climate-material risk flagged before specification is finalised
6. Client presentation | SAT: Export with full source citations — authoritative source requirement met by design

Time saved: New geographies (removes relationship network dependency); QC failures (team consistency enforced); material risk (prevented upstream); client sign-off (citations built in)
